**Q:** Impaired gastric emptying is reported a lot for semaglutide — does the label already cover it?

Yes, the label does cover gastric emptying impairment, but FAERS is showing substantial reporting for it.

**What FAERS reports:** 3,101 cases name semaglutide as a suspect drug and report impaired gastric emptying (across 2025–2026 Q1–Q2).

**What the label says:** The semaglutide labels describe gastric emptying delay in multiple places:

- [OZEMPIC injection, version 20, Clinical Pharmacology (12.2)]: "Semaglutide causes a delay of early postprandial gastric emptying, thereby reducing the rate at which glucose appears in the circulation postprandially."

- [OZEMPIC injection, version 20, Warnings and Precautions (5.10)]: "OZEMPIC delays gastric emptying" — noted specifically in the context of pulmonary aspiration risk during general anesthesia or deep sedation.

- [RYBELSUS/Ozempic tablet, version 14, Clinical Pharmacology (12.2)]: "Semaglutide causes a delay of early postprandial gastric emptying, thereby reducing the rate at which glucose appears in the circulation postprandially."

The label explicitly describes gastric emptying delay as a pharmacological effect and mentions it in warnings about perioperative safety and drug interactions. The high case count reflects that this is a recognized and reported phenomenon, and it is already addressed in the prescribing information.
