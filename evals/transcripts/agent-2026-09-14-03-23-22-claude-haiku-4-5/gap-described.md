Yes, **impaired gastric emptying is already described in the label**. 

The FAERS data show **1,073 cases** of impaired gastric emptying reported for semaglutide, with a reporting odds ratio of 68.02 (95% CI: 60.67–76.27) versus all other suspect drugs — a high disproportionality.

However, this is not a new or missed safety signal. The label explicitly describes it:

- **[OZEMPIC (SEMAGLUTIDE) INJECTION, SOLUTION, version 20, Clinical Pharmacology section]**: "Semaglutide causes a delay of early postprandial gastric emptying, thereby reducing the rate at which glucose appears in the circulation postprandially."

- **[OZEMPIC (SEMAGLUTIDE) INJECTION, SOLUTION, version 20, Warnings and Precautions 5.10]**: "OZEMPIC delays gastric emptying [see Clinical Pharmacology (12.2)]." This warning specifically addresses the clinical consequence — pulmonary aspiration risk during general anesthesia or deep sedation in patients with residual gastric contents.

The label also notes in Clinical Pharmacology that "The delay of gastric emptying with semaglutide may influence the absorption of concomitantly administered oral medications."

So while impaired gastric emptying is indeed frequently reported, it is an established and described pharmacological effect of semaglutide that is appropriately covered in the current prescribing information.
