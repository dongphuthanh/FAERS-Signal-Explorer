Yes, the label covers it. **1,073 cases** of impaired gastric emptying are reported to FAERS for semaglutide (ROR 68.02, 95% CI 60.67–76.27), and the label **describes** this finding.

The label discusses gastric emptying in the **Clinical Pharmacology** section:

- [OZEMPIC (SEMAGLUTIDE) INJECTION, version 20, *Clinical Pharmacology*]: "Semaglutide causes a delay of early postprandial gastric emptying, thereby reducing the rate at which glucose appears in the circulation postprandially."

It also warns about the clinical consequence in the **Warnings and Precautions** section:

- [OZEMPIC (SEMAGLUTIDE) INJECTION, version 20, *Warnings and Precautions* 5.10]: "OZEMPIC delays gastric emptying. There have been rare postmarketing reports of pulmonary aspiration in patients receiving GLP-1 receptor agonists undergoing elective surgeries or procedures requiring general anesthesia or deep sedation who had residual gastric contents despite reported adherence to preoperative fasting recommendations."

The label also notes in the **Drug Interactions** section that this delay may affect oral medication absorption, though clinical trials showed no clinically relevant interactions.

So impaired gastric emptying is well-characterized in the label—it is a described pharmacological action, with a specific warning about aspiration risk during anesthesia.
